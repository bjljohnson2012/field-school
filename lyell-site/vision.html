Method

How we build companies

Find the complex environment. Understand what is actually driving the people in it. Build a system that works inside those constraints instead of pretending they do not exist.

That sentence is Ben’s. Lyell exists so it can become a house, not a speech. Field School is the first company built that way in public.

Incentives over features

If behavior looks irrational, the incentives are misunderstood. A buyer has a mandate, a budget cycle, and a career risk calculation. Selling the feature set misses all three.

Systems, not heroics

Repeatable process beats a brilliant week. The gap between good judgment and consistent execution of it is a systems problem. Systems problems are solvable.

First principles, then the shop

Break the problem into mechanics. Name constraints. Map incentives. Then install the shop that works in that room, not the generic one.

What we refuse

A generic AI agency. A fourth product because the first three are unfinished. More founder hours as the scale plan. A company that only works while someone heroic is in the chair. Lyell’s job is to hold the plot and kill extra scope. The school stays the school.

Companies are moral objects

Every shop already runs on assumptions. Those assumptions sit under how you hire, quote, promise, and say yes. Unnamed assumptions become habits. Habits become systems. Systems become the company.

Dominion here is stewardship of what has been entrusted: mind, money, work, words, tools, and obligations. We name the assumption before we spend. If it is false, we name the true one and act from that. If it cannot be named, we wait a day.

The point is not a prettier holding page. The point is companies that last because they tell the truth about the room they are in.
